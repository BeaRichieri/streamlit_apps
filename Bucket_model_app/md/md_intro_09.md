| **Stage 1: Narrow fractures** | **Stage 2: Fracture enlargement** | **Stage 3: Conduit development** |
|---|---|---|
| Water enters small tectonic fractures or bedding-plane openings. Flow is limited, and initial enlargement is slow. | Dissolution gradually widens preferential pathways, allowing more water to pass through them. | Increasing flow promotes further dissolution and may produce conduits, caves, and organised drainage networks. |

This development creates a positive feedback:

**Larger fractures → more flow → more dissolution → further fracture enlargement**

Differences in flow and dissolution rates lead to hierarchically organised networks of pores, fractures, conduits, and caves. These networks are hydraulically connected and commonly drain towards one or several major karst springs.

> **Key message**  
> Karstification is a self-reinforcing process: once preferential pathways begin to enlarge, increasing flow can accelerate conduit development.
