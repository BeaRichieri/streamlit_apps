| Process | Slower component | Faster component |
|---|---|---|
| **Recharge** | Diffuse infiltration through soil, epikarst, pores, and small fractures | Concentrated recharge through sinkholes, sinking streams, and open fractures |
| **Storage** | Relatively large storage in the matrix, fractures, and epikarst, with gradual release | Relatively limited storage in conduits, with rapid filling and drainage |
| **Flow** | Slow, generally laminar flow through pores and smaller fractures | Fast flow through large fractures and conduits, which may become turbulent |
| **Discharge** | During recession and dry periods, spring flow may be sustained by slower drainage from the matrix, fractures, and epikarst | During precipitation events, spring discharge may be dominated by rapid conduit flow and can rise within hours or days |

> **Key message**  
> The coexistence of slow storage and flow in the matrix–fracture domain and rapid flow in conduits is a defining characteristic of karst aquifers. This behaviour motivates conceptual models that represent these domains separately.
