The **conduit-to-spring flux** $Q_{\mathrm{CS}}$ (m³/$\Delta t$) represents the faster spring-discharge contribution, whereas the **matrix-to-spring flux** $Q_{\mathrm{MS}}$ (m³/$\Delta t$) represents a slower contribution.

The total simulated **spring discharge** $Q_{\mathrm{spring}}$ (m³/$\Delta t$) is the sum of the conduit-to-spring flux and matrix-to-spring flux.

**Symbols in these equations**

| Symbol | Unit | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| $R_a$ | m² | Total recharge area. |
| 🔵 $k_{\mathrm{CS}}$ | mm/$\Delta t$ | Discharge coefficient controlling drainage from the conduit toward the spring. |
| 🔵 $a_{\mathrm{CS}}$ | – | Dimensionless exponent controlling the nonlinearity of conduit drainage. |
| 🔵 $k_{\mathrm{MS}}$ | mm/$\Delta t$ | Discharge coefficient controlling drainage from the matrix toward the spring. |
| 🔵 $a_{\mathrm{MS}}$ | – | Dimensionless exponent controlling the nonlinearity of matrix drainage. |
| $C_t$ | – | Dimensionless water level in the conduit. |
| $M_t$ | – | Dimensionless water level in the matrix. |
