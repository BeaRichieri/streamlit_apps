Sustainable management of karst aquifers is therefore essential for maintaining groundwater quantity and quality for drinking-water supply, agriculture, ecosystems, industry, and other societal needs.

| **Extensive** | **Essential** | **Vulnerable** |
|---|---|---|
| Karst landscapes occupy a substantial proportion of the Earth's land surface and of Europe. | Karst aquifers provide freshwater to a large part of the global population. | Rapid recharge and preferential flow paths can transport contaminants through the aquifer with limited attenuation. |

### Why are karst aquifers particularly vulnerable?

Karst aquifers are highly vulnerable to contamination because water can enter and move through them rapidly. Preferential flow paths—including fractures, sinkholes, sinking streams, and conduits—can transport pollutants over large distances within short periods.

In many karst regions, soils are thin, discontinuous, or locally absent. Pollutants released at the land surface may therefore reach the aquifer with limited filtration, sorption, or degradation.

| **Rapid transport** | **Limited protection** | **Temporary storage** |
|---|---|---|
| Connected fractures and conduits allow contaminants to spread quickly through the aquifer. | Thin soils and exposed carbonate rocks may provide only limited natural protection. | Contaminants may enter the surrounding matrix during high-flow conditions and be released gradually later. |

### Matrix–conduit exchange and contaminant persistence

During high-flow events, hydraulic gradients may cause water from karst conduits to enter the surrounding rock matrix. If the conduit water contains contaminants, part of the contaminant mass may also be transferred into the matrix.

After the event, the hydraulic gradient may reverse. Contaminated water stored temporarily in the matrix can then be released gradually back into the conduit network. Consequently, a short contamination event may cause a much longer period of impaired water quality at a karst spring.

> **Key challenge**  
> Rapid conduit transport can spread contamination quickly, while temporary storage in the surrounding matrix can prolong contaminant release long after the original pollution event has ended.

### Increasing pressures on karst groundwater

Karst groundwater resources are increasingly affected by climatic and anthropogenic pressures. These pressures can reduce groundwater availability, alter recharge conditions, and degrade water quality.

| **Climate-related pressures** | **Anthropogenic pressures** |
|---|---|
| Changing precipitation patterns; more frequent or prolonged droughts; more intense rainfall events; changes in groundwater recharge | Unsustainable groundwater abstraction; agricultural and urban pollution; changes in land cover and land use; urbanisation and infrastructure development |

> **Management need**  
> Sustainable karst-water management requires an understanding of both rapid conduit responses and slower storage and release processes.
