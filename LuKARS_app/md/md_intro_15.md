| **Epikarst** | **Matrix and fractures** | **Conduits** |
|---|---|---|
| The highly weathered uppermost carbonate-rock zone beneath the soil. It can store recharge temporarily, redistribute it, and regulate its transfer to deeper parts of the aquifer. | The rock volume represented by matrix pores and smaller fractures. It commonly provides a large share of groundwater storage and supports relatively slow flow. | Dissolution-enlarged fractures and channels. They transmit water rapidly towards karst springs and other discharge points. |

Recharge can be routed downward through two broad types of pathway:

- **Rapid transfer** towards open fractures and the conduit network
- **Slower, diffuse infiltration** towards the matrix and smaller fractures

Differences in hydraulic head can also cause exchange between the matrix–fracture domain and the conduit network. Water may move from the matrix towards the conduits or, under some conditions, from conduits into the surrounding fractured rock.
