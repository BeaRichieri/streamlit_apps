| **Matrix porosity** | **Fracture porosity** | **Conduit porosity** |
|---|---|---|
| Small intergranular or intragranular pores within the rock matrix. They generally support slow groundwater movement and can provide substantial storage. | Tectonic fractures, joints, and bedding-plane discontinuities. They connect parts of the rock matrix and may form pathways towards larger conduits. | Dissolution-enlarged fractures, channels, and caves. They can permit very rapid groundwater transport. |

The coexistence of these domains produces strongly **heterogeneous and anisotropic hydraulic properties**. Hydraulic conductivity and groundwater velocity can vary by several orders of magnitude over short distances.

##### Hydraulic-conductivity ranges

| Material or system | Approximate hydraulic conductivity, $K$ (m/day) |
|---|---:|
| Clay | $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-2}$ |
| Silt | $10^{-2}$ to $1$ |
| Sand | $10^{-1}$ to $10^{2}$ |
| Gravel | $10^{1}$ to $10^{4}$ |
| Karst system | $10^{1}$ to more than $10^{6}$ |

*These ranges are indicative only. Values depend on material structure, measurement scale, fracture connectivity, and conduit development. A karst system is not a uniform material; reported effective conductivities may represent the combined behaviour of matrix, fractures, and conduits.*

> **Key message**  
> Karst systems combine relatively low-permeability rock volumes with highly transmissive fractures and conduits.
