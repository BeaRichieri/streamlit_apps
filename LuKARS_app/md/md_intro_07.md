Carbonate rocks have relatively low solubility in pure water, but their dissolution increases strongly when carbon dioxide ($\mathrm{CO_2}$) is present. Carbon dioxide enters water from the atmosphere and from biological activity in the soil. Important soil sources include plant-root respiration, microbial activity, and the decomposition of organic matter.

When carbon dioxide dissolves in water, it contributes to the formation of carbonic acid:

$$
\mathrm{CO_2 + H_2O \rightleftharpoons H_2CO_3}
$$

Carbonic acid is a weak acid, but it substantially increases the dissolution of carbonate minerals.

##### Calcite dissolution

$$
\mathrm{CaCO_3 + CO_2 + H_2O \rightleftharpoons Ca^{2+} + 2HCO_3^-}
$$


##### Dolomite dissolution

$$
\mathrm{CaMg(CO_3)_2 + 2CO_2 + 2H_2O \rightleftharpoons Ca^{2+} + Mg^{2+} + 4HCO_3^-}
$$


These reactions influence the chemical composition of groundwater emerging at karst springs. Groundwater that has interacted for instance with dolostone is commonly rich in calcium, magnesium, and bicarbonate and may therefore be classified as a **Ca–Mg–HCO₃ groundwater type**.

> **Key message**  
> Carbon dioxide dissolved in water promotes carbonate dissolution and therefore plays a central role in karstification.
