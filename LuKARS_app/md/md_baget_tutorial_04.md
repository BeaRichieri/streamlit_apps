#### Exercise 4 - Conduit release to the spring

### Mechanism

For the Baget reference setup, the conduit contribution to the spring is

$$
Q_{\mathrm{CS},t}
=
R_a
k_{\mathrm{CS}}
C_t^{a_{\mathrm{CS}}}
$$

`kCS` and `aCS` therefore do not play the same role: one scales the release coefficient, while the other controls the nonlinear dependence on conduit storage.

### A. Release coefficient — `kCS`

#### Predict

If conduit storage were identical, what should increasing `kCS` do to `QCS`?

#### Experiment

1. Start from the **Reference** simulation.
2. Change only `kCS`.
3. Compare a lower and a higher value on the logarithmic slider.

#### Inspect

Compare:

- conduit storage;
- `QCS`;
- spring peak;
- recession after rainfall events.

**Guiding question:** When the conduit releases water more efficiently, how do conduit storage and the spring hydrograph respond together?

---

### B. Nonlinear storage-discharge relation — `aCS`

#### Predict

`aCS` is an exponent. Rather than assuming that a larger value always means a larger discharge, predict **which parts of the hydrograph may be most affected**.

#### Experiment

1. Reset to the reference.
2. Change only `aCS`.
3. Compare a lower and a higher value.

#### Inspect

Focus on differences between:

- high-flow periods;
- intermediate flow;
- recession and low-flow periods.

### Interpret

**Guiding question:** How is modifying the curvature of the conduit storage-discharge relationship different from simply changing the release coefficient `kCS`?
